Chapter IX

The Offer

She took him into the garden while the hotel slept. Purple roses. A stone fountain. The castle rising behind them.

“I will not take what you have not given,” Isolde said. They stood close, her hands in his, forehead nearly against his. “But I can offer what the night offered me. Not death. Only this: that the dawn will forget you the same way it has forgotten me.”

Elias looked at her mouth, then at the place where his tattoos met his throat.

“Will it hurt?”

“Yes. Then it will not. Then it will feel like coming home.”

He lifted her gloved hand and pressed his lips to the lace at her wrist.

“Then do it.”

When her mouth found the place where his neck met his shoulder, it was almost tender. The night leaned in around them.

When she drew back, his eyes were still his. Only brighter. Certain.

“Now you belong to the Veil,” she whispered. “And the Veil belongs to you.”
