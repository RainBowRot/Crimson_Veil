Chapter IX

The Offer

She took him into the garden while the hotel pretended to sleep. Purple roses. A stone fountain. The castle rising behind them like a witness that had already signed the papers.

Seraphine

“I will not take what you have not given,” Seraphine said. They stood close, her hands in his, forehead nearly against his. “This is the true offer, not the pretty one. If I bite you, you will belong to night. Hunger will come. Sunlight will become a story you tell with your mouth and not your skin. The Obsidian will try to keep you as property. I am offering to keep you as mine so the house cannot have you alone. I will be your door. You will be the first person I have allowed to be a door for me. If we fail, you will go into the ledger anyway, and I will have to walk past the glass.”

Cassian

He looked at her mouth, then at the place where his tattoos met his throat, the clock she had named.

“Will it hurt?”

“Yes. Then it will not. Then it will feel like coming home, which is a more dangerous pain. Home is how the house argues.”

He thought of the ferry ticket. The blank page. The corridor that had worn his footprint before he walked. He thought of her shaking after she did not drink him, and of the word treason.

He lifted her gloved hand and pressed his lips to the lace at her wrist.

“Then do it. Not for the hotel. For you. If I belong to anything, I belong to the woman who stopped.”

The roses leaned in. The fountain forgot to fall.

When her mouth found the place where his neck met his shoulder, it was almost tender. It was also a theft she had finally asked for. The night leaned around them. He held her skull, careful of the pins, and let the hurt arrive, bright as a needle, familiar as work.

He did not go quiet. That was the victory. He stayed loud inside his own name while the dark rewrote the rest.

When she drew back, his eyes were still his. Only brighter. Certain. The fountain started again, as if embarrassed.

“Now you belong to the Obsidian,” she whispered, and then, fiercely, correcting the script the stones wanted, “and the Obsidian belongs to you. I am the Obsidian it does not get to speak without.”

Below them, in the black stone, something old knocked twice, and then — for the first time since the Lady of the Cliff had stopped being seen in daylight — went still.
