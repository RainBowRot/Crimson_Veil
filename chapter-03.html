Chapter III

The Pulse

They stood together at the great window. Below them the sea tore itself against the rocks.

Isolde raised one gloved hand and rested her fingers against the side of Elias’s throat, right where the tattoos began. His pulse jumped beneath her touch — fast, living, mortal.

“This,” she murmured, “is the only clock that still matters here.”

He could have stepped back. He did not.

“One night,” she offered. “That is all I am asking. One night where you stop pretending you want the morning.”

His voice was rough. “And if one night isn’t enough?”

Her smile was slow, sharp, and almost kind.

“Then the night will simply refuse to end.”
