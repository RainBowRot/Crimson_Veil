Chapter III

The Pulse

They stood together at the great window. Below them the sea tore itself against the rocks as if it, too, had been told it could not leave.

Seraphine

She raised one gloved hand and rested her fingers against the side of his throat, right where the ink began. His pulse jumped — fast, living, mortal. A clock with no permission to stop.

This was the part the house liked. The counting.

Seraphine’s private rule was older than her name: she did not drink what had not been offered. The house had no such rule. It drank hours. It drank mornings. It had drunk every guest who had smiled at her and then gone quiet in a room that would not open from the inside.

She could have lied. She did not.

Cassian

“This,” she murmured, “is the only clock that still matters here.”

He could have stepped back. He felt the space behind his heels, the polite inch of freedom she was leaving him, and understood it was a test he would grade himself on for the rest of his life.

He did not step back.

“What happens to the ones who stay?”

“Some become the quiet in the walls.” Her glove was cool. She did not press. “Some I am allowed to keep as themselves. I have not been allowed that in a long time. The hotel is greedy. I am trying, tonight, to be worse than greedy. I am trying to be particular.”

“One night,” she offered. “That is all I am asking in a language you still trust. One night where you stop pretending you want the morning.”

His voice was rough. “And if one night isn’t enough?”

Her smile was slow, sharp, and almost kind. Kindness on that mouth looked like a weapon that had learned manners.

“Then the night will simply refuse to end.”

He put his hand over hers at his throat. Not to pull her off. To keep her there, and to make the keeping his idea.

“If I stay the night,” he said, “you tell me what the locked floor is.”

Her red eyes flicked. So he had seen it. Good. She preferred him dangerous to her secrets.

“Survive until what should be dawn,” Seraphine said, “and I will show you what the house does with people who only wanted shelter.”
