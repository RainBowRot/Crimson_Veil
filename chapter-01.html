Chapter I

The Hotel on the Cliff

Cassian

The Obsidian Hotel had no need of signs. Travelers who were meant to find it simply did. It rose from the Cornish cliffs like something that had grown there rather than been built — black stone, tall windows glowing with candlelight, and a constant veil of sea fog that never quite lifted.

Cassian arrived on the third night of rain with a ferry ticket in his coat that he had already missed twice. Dark hair damp, full beard shadowed, the tattoos that climbed his throat looking darker in the hotel’s low light. He told himself he would stay only until the weather cleared. He told himself that in every town. Leaving was the one skill he had finished learning.

The clerk at the desk was a woman with no name tag and a smile that did not reach her eyes. She did not ask how long. She set a key on the wood as if it had been waiting.

“Room nine,” she said. “The house prefers odd numbers.”

The corridor to nine was longer than the building. Portraits watched him pass with the patience of furniture. In the stairwell a clock had gold hands and no numbers. It ticked anyway.

His room faced the sea. He unpacked nothing except the needle case he carried out of habit — ink, steel, a small bottle of black. On his chest, under the unbuttoned shirt, a map of roads he had not stayed to finish. He sat on the edge of the bed and listened to the rain fail to sound like rain. It sounded like a held breath.

When he went back toward the lobby to ask about breakfast hours, the stairs delivered him to a different landing. A garden of purple roses showed through a tall window that had not been there. The roses were open. It was the wrong season for that.

He found the front door on the second try. The fog on the drive was so thick the gates were a rumor. He walked anyway, coat collar up, until the road bent and put the hotel in front of him again, lights warmer, as if pleased.

He stood in the wet and understood, without poetry, that leaving would take more than weather.

The hotel had other ideas. It always had.
