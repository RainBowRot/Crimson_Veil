Chapter VI

What the Night Takes

She undressed him the way one opens a gift that has been waited for, and also the way one checks a lock. When his shirt fell away the full map of his tattoos showed — neck, chest, arms — dark and deliberate against warm skin. Roads. Dates. A ferry he had not boarded. A name small enough to be a bruise, lettered over the sternum, that he did not explain.

He returned the favor, sliding the lace from her shoulders until the gown pooled at her waist. She was cold the way marble is cold in a church you still want to kneel in. He warmed her with his mouth at the hollow of her throat, and she made a sound the house had not earned.

She leaned in until their foreheads touched.

“Tell me to stop,” she said, “and I will. That rule is mine. The house does not get a vote in this room if you speak.”

His answer was quiet. “Don’t stop. Don’t let it in either.”

So she didn’t. Either.

Seraphine

What followed was deliberate. A slow claiming of breath and pulse and the particular heat of a man who had decided, fully, to stay. She taught him the difference between being taken and being chosen: she asked, with her hands, before she took the weight of him; she waited for the yes in his fingers at her hips; she did not bite when the hunger rose, even though his throat was a lit window and she had not eaten anything that mattered in longer than she would admit.

She almost failed. Her mouth opened on the ink at his shoulder. His blood would have been a door she could not close. He said her name — Seraphine, not Obsidian, not my lady — and the name landed like a hand on her jaw.

She stopped. The stopping cost her. Her hands shook on his ribs. The house, somewhere below, knocked once in the pipes, displeased.

Cassian

He felt the almost-bite like a storm turning. He pulled her down and finished what they had started with his body instead of her teeth, until she came apart quiet and furious in his arms, and he followed, mortal and ridiculous and still, somehow, in charge of the next minute.

After, she hid her face against the map on his chest.

“I have not stopped for anyone,” she said into his skin. “If I drink you, the hotel may write you into the walls. If I do not, I will want you anyway. That is not the bargain I was built for.”

He stroked the ruined updo. “Then we are both off the map.”
