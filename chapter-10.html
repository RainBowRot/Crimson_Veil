Chapter X

One Eternal Night

They did not return to the road.

There was no road left that would have them, and, more importantly, no road they owed.

Cassian

In the time that was no longer measured by sun, Cassian learned the quiet work of the hotel: which doors were hungry, which clocks were ashamed, which rooms could be opened now that he had a name the walls had failed to file. Seraphine taught him the difference between hunger and want. He taught her what it felt like to be chosen by someone who could still remember sunlight, even if remembering was all the sunlight he was allowed.

The ledger room they gutted together. The stains remained. The blank page they burned. Ash does not bring anyone back. It does stop a house from pretending the next line is inevitable.

Seraphine

Sometimes they danced in the empty ballroom. Sometimes they stood at the cliff’s edge and let the fog erase the world. Always they came back to each other — her pale hand against the dark ink on his chest, his tattooed fingers carefully cradling the back of her updo as if the style were a country he had decided, at last, to inhabit.

Guests still found the gates, on nights the fog was generous. Seraphine met them at the desk herself. She offered a room until the weather cleared, and she meant the weather. Cassian stood in the stairwell and listened for corridors that tried to loop. When they did, he put his palm to the wallpaper and said, as if to a bad dog, “No.”

The Obsidian Hotel no longer waited for travelers the way a mouth waits.

It had keepers.

And so did they: each other, which was the only forever that had been asked for out loud.
